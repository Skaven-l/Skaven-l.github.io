Eastern Turkey | a land of volcanic mountains and ancient landscapes

Eastern Turkey is a region of dramatic contrasts, where enormous volcanic mountains, vast high-altitude plateaus, deep valleys and remote villages create some of the country's most impressive landscapes. Close to the borders of Armenia and Iran, the region is dominated by Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı), a massive dormant volcano rising 5,137 metres above sea level and the highest mountain in Turkey.

Among its most remarkable sights are Mount Ararat – famous for its snow-covered summit and biblical associations; Ishak Pasha Palace – a spectacular 18th-century palace overlooking the mountains near Doğubayazıt; and Lake Van – an enormous turquoise-coloured salt lake surrounded by volcanic landscapes. Further north, Mount Tendürek and the Muradiye Waterfalls offer striking natural scenery, while the ancient city of Ani features the impressive ruins of medieval churches and fortifications.

Itinerary

Day 1 – Arrival in Van and Lake Van

Arrive at Van Ferit Melen Airport and transfer to your hotel in Van. Spend the afternoon exploring Lake Van, Turkey's largest lake, known for its deep turquoise waters and mountainous surroundings. Visit Van Castle, an ancient Urartian fortress built on a rocky ridge overlooking the lake. Overnight in a hotel in Van.

Day 2 – Muradiye Waterfalls and Mount Ararat

Leave Van and travel north towards Doğubayazıt, stopping at Muradiye Waterfalls along the way. Here, the Bendimahi River drops over a series of rocky formations, creating a beautiful waterfall surrounded by green landscapes. Continue towards Doğubayazıt, where Mount Ararat gradually appears across the open plateau. Overnight in a guesthouse in Doğubayazıt.

Day 3 – Ishak Pasha Palace and Mount Ararat

Explore Ishak Pasha Palace, an impressive 18th-century complex built on a rocky hillside overlooking the surrounding plains. Its architecture combines Ottoman, Persian and Seljuk influences, with spectacular views of Mount Ararat in the distance. Afterwards, visit the surrounding historical ruins and enjoy the mountain scenery from accessible viewpoints around Doğubayazıt. Overnight in Doğubayazıt.

Day 4 – Diyadin Canyon and Thermal Springs

Travel towards Diyadin to discover its unusual geological landscapes. Explore Diyadin Canyon, where the Murat River has carved its way through steep rocky formations. Afterwards, visit the nearby thermal springs, where naturally heated water emerges from underground. Enjoy the surrounding highland scenery before returning to Doğubayazıt for the night.

Day 5 – Return to Van and departure

Begin the journey back towards Van, following the open landscapes and mountain scenery of eastern Anatolia. Allow approximately 3 hours or more for the drive, depending on stops and road conditions. Arrive at Van Airport and catch a domestic flight to Istanbul, where you can connect with your international journey home.
